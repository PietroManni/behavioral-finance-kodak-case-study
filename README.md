# Behavioral Finance – Eastman Kodak (KODK) July 2020 Case Study

This repository contains my Behavioral Finance project report analyzing the July 2020 Eastman Kodak (KODK) market episode through a behavioral finance lens.

The analysis focuses on how a highly salient news shock (U.S. government-backed loan narrative) interacted with investor attention, media/social amplification, and cognitive/emotional biases, leading to extreme short-run price fluctuations followed by a sharp reversal and partial stabilization.

## Contents

- `Behavioral_Finance.pdf` — final report (PDF)

## Report structure (as required by the assignment)

### Part A — Analyzing price variations
- Price evolution over the studied period
- Absolute and percentage variations
- Short-term vs long-term impacts
- Recovery vs capitalization of gains
- Volatility, trading activity, and market capitalization changes  
  *(Bloomberg charts: price, volume, market cap, historical volatility)*

### Part B — Firm fundamentals and underlying causes
- Brief fundamentals check around the event (profitability, liquidity, credit/leverage)
- Internal and external triggers + illustrative timeline
- News and social media coverage: attention, sentiment, amplification  
  *(Bloomberg proxies: headlines list, overall news attention, Twitter publications/sentiment)*

### Part C — Impacts of biases on investors’ behavior
- Most relevant cognitive and emotional biases (class classification)
- Mechanisms linking biases to trading behavior (GameStop-style analysis)
- How biases amplified price dynamics (upward and downward)

### Part D — Analysis and comparison to GameStop
- Rational vs irrational drivers
- Key lessons for investors
- Debiasing strategies / risk controls
- Similarities and differences vs GameStop (drivers, amplitude, role of platforms, short squeeze mechanics)
